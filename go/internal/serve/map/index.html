What does a gap mean?

The tint shows the share of satellites above each location’s horizon with fresh navigation observations in this audience. Red means below 50% observed; amber means 50–80%. The tint clears when at least 80% meet your station target. These are planning thresholds, not a promise of perfect reception.

A single station can provide useful coverage without hearing every satellite. Selecting a two-station target also makes areas amber where at least 50% are observed but fewer than 80% have two reporting stations. The map checks whether satellites visible at each location are heard anywhere in this audience; it does not draw a reception radius around your station. Inspect a location to see the observed and missing counts.

Coverage means monitoring of the reference satellites. It does not measure local interference, positioning accuracy, every signal, or satellite health. Two reporting stations do not prove their data agree.

Reference orbits and missing data

BKG’s independent broadcast-orbit reference locates satellites your collector may never have heard. An expired orbit remains listed as unknown; it does not disappear and improve the map. Hatching marks uncertainty even where known gaps are also visible.

The reference may omit satellites too. The percentages use selected satellites with known positions at this time. Hatching means other satellites may be visible too; a clear tint does not guarantee complete GNSS service. SBAS, NavIC, and experimental GLONASS slots are outside this view.
