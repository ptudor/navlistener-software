What does a gap mean?

Red areas have at least one visible satellite with no fresh navigation observations in this audience. Amber areas have observations, but fewer stations than your target.

A satellite can be observed from far beyond its point on this map. Inspect a possible station location to see which missing satellites are above its horizon.

Coverage means monitoring of the reference satellites. It does not measure local interference, positioning accuracy, every signal, or satellite health. Two reporting stations do not prove their data agree.

Reference orbits and missing data

BKG’s independent broadcast-orbit reference locates satellites your collector may never have heard. An expired orbit remains listed as unknown; it does not disappear and improve the map. Hatching marks uncertainty even where known gaps are also visible.

The reference may omit satellites too. “Target met” applies to the selected reference catalogue at this time, not a guarantee of complete GNSS service. SBAS, NavIC, and experimental GLONASS slots are outside this view.
